Finite Differences and Operators

Data in a table, no formula in sight: difference tables, the operators Δ, ∇, δ, E, μ and D, where each one comes from, how they are related, and why E = e^{hD} is the key to every interpolation, differentiation and integration formula that follows.

Why this lecture

The problem: a table with no formula

A machine logs one reading per second. We have only the numbers.

  • Is there a polynomial behind the data? Of which degree?
  • What was the value at t=2.5t = 2.5t=2.5 s, between two readings?
  • One entry looks odd. Is it a recording error? Which one?
  • All three questions are answered by one tool: repeated subtraction of neighbouring values

Differences of a polynomial

For a polynomial of degree n with leading coefficient aΔn(axn+⋯)=a n! hn  (a constant),Δn+1=0\Delta^{n}\big(a x^n + \cdots\big) = a\,n!\,h^n \;(\text{a constant}), \qquad \Delta^{n+1} = 0Δn(axn+⋯)=an!hn(a constant),Δn+1=0
Why: each Δ lowers the degree by oneΔxn=(x+h)n−xn=nh xn−1+⋯\Delta x^n = (x+h)^n - x^n = n h\,x^{n-1} + \cdotsΔxn=(x+h)n−xn=nhxn−1+⋯
  • Opening data: Δ3y=6=a⋅3!⋅13\Delta^3 y = 6 = a \cdot 3! \cdot 1^3Δ3y=6=a⋅3!⋅13, so a=1a = 1a=1: a cubic t3+⋯t^3 + \cdotst3+⋯
  • This is how we "see" the degree in a table
  • Real data: differences become small and noisy rather than exactly zero
  • Example: with h=1h = 1h=1, Δx3=3x2+3x+1\Delta x^3 = 3x^2 + 3x + 1Δx3=3x2+3x+1 and Δ2x3=6x+6\Delta^2 x^3 = 6x + 6Δ2x3=6x+6

Where the relations come from

Express every operator in terms of EEE, using only the definitions.
1
Δf(x)=f(x+h)−f(x)=Ef(x)−f(x)\Delta f(x) = f(x+h) - f(x) = Ef(x) - f(x)Δf(x)=f(x+h)−f(x)=Ef(x)−f(x), so Δ=E−1\boxed{\Delta = E - 1}Δ=E−1​, i.e. E=1+ΔE = 1 + \DeltaE=1+Δ
2
∇f(x)=f(x)−f(x−h)=f(x)−E−1f(x)\nabla f(x) = f(x) - f(x-h) = f(x) - E^{-1}f(x)∇f(x)=f(x)−f(x−h)=f(x)−E−1f(x), so ∇=1−E−1\boxed{\nabla = 1 - E^{-1}}∇=1−E−1​

4

Using differences to check data

Finding an error in a table

  • Add an error ε\varepsilonε to one entry: it spreads through the table
  • In the kkk-th differences it appears as ε\varepsilonε times binomial coefficients with alternating signs
  • Third differences become 6+ε(1,−3,3,−1)=7,3,9,56 + \varepsilon(1, -3, 3, -1) = 7, 3, 9, 56+ε(1,−3,3,−1)=7,3,9,5
  • The pattern points to the wrong entry; subtract ε\varepsilonε to correct it

Classical vs numerical

Same question, two approaches: "what is yyy at t=2.5t = 2.5t=2.5?"

Algebra

When the classical approach cannot work

Real measurements: no formula exists

Census counts, temperatures or stock prices are not a polynomial; guessing "the formula" is impossible. The difference table works directly on the numbers, tells us how smooth the data are, and gives interpolation (Lecture 16), derivatives (Lecture 19) and integrals (Lecture 20).
