Taylor Series and Order of Accuracy

Almost every formula in this course comes from a Taylor series cut off after a few terms. The first neglected term tells us the error, and "O(h²)" tells us how fast it shrinks.

Today's goals

  • Write the Taylor series of a function and its remainder term

Step form and big-O notation

Taylor series with step hf(x+h)=f(x)+hf′(x)+h22f′′(x)+h36f′′′(x)+⋯f(x+h) = f(x) + h f'(x) + \frac{h^2}{2} f''(x) + \frac{h^3}{6} f'''(x) + \cdotsf(x+h)=f(x)+hf′(x)+2h2​f′′(x)+6h3​f′′′(x)+⋯
Big-OE(h)=O(hp)  ⟺  ∣E(h)∣≤C hp for small hE(h) = O(h^p) \iff |E(h)| \le C\,h^p \text{ for small } hE(h)=O(hp)⟺∣E(h)∣≤Chp for small h
Order of accuracyE(h/2)≈E(h)/2p⇒p≈log⁡2E(h)E(h/2)E(h/2) \approx E(h)/2^{p} \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad p \approx \log_2 \frac{E(h)}{E(h/2)}E(h/2)≈E(h)/2p⇒p≈log2​E(h/2)E(h)​
  • O(h)O(h)O(h): halve hhh → error halves (first order)
  • O(h2)O(h^2)O(h2): halve hhh → error ÷ 4 (second order)
  • O(h4)O(h^4)O(h4): halve hhh → error ÷ 16 (RK4, Simpson)
  • Higher order = far fewer steps for the same accuracy

Beyond the classroom

Where this shows up in CSE and machine learning

⬇️

Gradient descent is first-order Taylor

L(w+Δ)≈L(w)+∇LTΔL(w + \Delta) \approx L(w) + \nabla L^{T}\DeltaL(w+Δ)≈L(w)+∇LTΔ. The decrease is largest for Δ=−η∇L\Delta = -\eta\nabla LΔ=−η∇L: that is the update rule (Lecture 25).

🎯

Newton's method is second-order Taylor

Adding 12ΔTHΔ\tfrac12\Delta^T H \Delta21​ΔTHΔ (the Hessian) and minimising gives Newton's step: the basis of fast optimisers.

🧮

Activation functions on chips

tanh⁡\tanhtanh, exe^xex and GELU are computed with short polynomials whose length comes from remainder bounds.

🔁

Backpropagation checks

Gradient checking compares backprop with L(w+h)−L(w−h)2h\frac{L(w+h)-L(w-h)}{2h}2hL(w+h)−L(w−h)​, the central difference, with h≈10−5h \approx 10^{-5}h≈10−5: the best step size, explained in Lecture 4.

Summary

Key points
  • Taylor: f(x)=∑f(k)(a)k!(x−a)k+Rnf(x) = \sum \frac{f^{(k)}(a)}{k!}(x-a)^k + R_nf(x)=∑k!f(k)(a)​(x−a)k+Rn​, with Rn=f(n+1)(ξ)(n+1)!(x−a)n+1R_n = \frac{f^{(n+1)}(\xi)}{(n+1)!}(x-a)^{n+1}Rn​=(n+1)!f(n+1)(ξ)​(x−a)n+1
  • Bound ∣f(n+1)∣|f^{(n+1)}|∣f(n+1)∣ to bound the truncation error; choose nnn from the bound
  • O(hp)O(h^p)O(hp): halving hhh divides the error by 2p2^p2p; measure ppp from two step sizes
  • Forward difference O(h)O(h)O(h); central difference O(h2)O(h^2)O(h2)
  • Every method later in the course (Newton, difference formulas, Simpson, Runge–Kutta) is a truncated Taylor series
