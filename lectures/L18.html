Spline Interpolation and the Runge Phenomenon

Why one polynomial through many equally spaced points can be disastrous, how Chebyshev points and piecewise polynomials fix it, and how a natural cubic spline is built from one tridiagonal system.

Pseudocode
input x[0..n], y[0..n], X
h[i] ← x[i+1] − x[i]
for i = 1 … n−1:          # tridiagonal rows

When splines are not enough

Limitations shown failing

  • Jumps: at a step the cubic spline overshoots (about 11% here) and wiggles; the linear spline does not
  • End conditions: "natural" (M=0M = 0M=0) is wrong if the true curve bends at the ends; errors are largest there
  • Noise: a spline still passes through every noisy point (use smoothing splines or least squares)
  • Extrapolation: outside the data the end cubic is a guess

When to use it

Cubic splines: method profile

Best case
  • Many points, smooth underlying curve: error ∼h4\sim h^4∼h4, no Runge swings
  • Any spacing; setup O(n)O(n)O(n) with the Thomas algorithm
  • Smooth slope and curvature: good for motion and shapes
Worst case
  • Jumps and sharp corners: overshoot
  • Noisy data: reproduces the noise exactly
  • Wrong end conditions: largest errors at the ends
Best suited for
  • Fonts, CAD, animation paths, robot trajectories
  • Resampling signals and filling gaps in data
  • Smooth look-up tables (physical properties, calibration curves)
Avoid when
  • Data with errors (use least squares or smoothing splines, Lecture 23)
  • Monotone data that must stay monotone (use PCHIP)
  • Extrapolation

Rule of thumb: few points → one polynomial (or Chebyshev); many points → cubic spline; noisy points → least squares.

Summary

Key points
  • Runge: one polynomial through many equally spaced points swings wildly near the ends
  • Chebyshev points tame it, if the points can be chosen
  • Splines: low-degree pieces joined smoothly; natural cubic spline from a tridiagonal system in MiM_iMi​
  • Cubic spline error falls like h4h^4h4; watch jumps, noise and end conditions
Opening problem: solved

With 11 equally spaced points the single polynomial has error 1.92; the natural cubic spline has 0.022, and with 21 points 0.003, while the polynomial grows to 60.
