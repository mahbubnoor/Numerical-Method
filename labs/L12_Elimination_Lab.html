Q3 What is partial pivoting and why is it needed?
Before eliminating column kkk, swap rows so that the entry of largest absolute value in column kkk (rows kkk to nnn) becomes the pivot. Then all multipliers satisfy ∣m∣≤1|m| \le 1∣m∣≤1, so rounding errors are not magnified; a tiny pivot (like 0.0001) would otherwise give multipliers like 10 000 and destroy the answer.

Q5 Compare Gaussian elimination and Gauss–Jordan elimination.
Gauss: zeros below the pivots, then back substitution; about 23n3\tfrac23 n^332​n3. Gauss–Jordan: zeros above and below, pivots scaled to 1, no back substitution; about n3n^3n3. Gauss–Jordan is convenient for inverses and RREF; Gauss is cheaper for solving.
